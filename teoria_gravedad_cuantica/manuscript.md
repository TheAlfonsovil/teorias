## Abstract

We present a discrete, information-theoretic model of spacetime in which geometry and gravitation emerge from the collective dynamics of quantized information. Spacetime is composed of sub-Planckian causal voxels that exchange information subject to a universal causal bound. The Planck scale is interpreted as a limit of measurability rather than a fundamental cutoff. We show that Einstein’s field equations arise in the macroscopic limit, with the gravitational constant appearing as an effective coupling determined by informational saturation and coarse-graining. Black holes correspond to regions of maximal informational density, leading naturally to horizons and area laws. Cosmological acceleration is interpreted as a large-scale informational gradient.

## 1. Introduction

General Relativity assumes a smooth spacetime manifold, while quantum theory implies discreteness and finite information capacity. The Planck scale is commonly interpreted as fundamental; however, it more properly represents a limit to operational measurability rather than an ontological minimum.

In this work, we propose that spacetime is fundamentally discrete at a scale \( l_{\min} \ll l_P \), and that gravity emerges from the redistribution of quantized information under causal constraints.

---

## 2. Fundamental Axioms

### Axiom I — Universal Causality

There exists a universal causal constant \( c \), representing the maximum speed of information propagation.

---

### Axiom II — Sub-Planckian Discrete Spacetime

Spacetime consists of elementary causal voxels characterized by:

- Minimal length: \( l_{\min} \)
- Minimal time: \( t_{\min} \)

with

\[
c = \frac{l_{\min}}{t_{\min}}
\]

The Planck length \( l_P \) arises as a coarse-grained, measurable scale, not as a fundamental minimum.

---

### Axiom III — Quantized Information

Each voxel contains discrete units of information. Information is a primitive physical quantity.

---

## 3. Elementary Information Units

Define the elementary informational unit:

\[
I_0 = \frac{1}{t_{\min}} = \frac{c}{l_{\min}}
\]

The information content of a voxel is quantized as:

\[
I(n) = n I_0, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}
\]

---

## 4. Maximal Informational Capacity and Flux

Each voxel admits a finite maximal information transfer rate:

\[
I_{\max} = \frac{c}{t_{\min}} = \frac{l_{\min}}{t_{\min}^2}
\]

This represents a fundamental saturation bound.

## 5. Informational Dynamics

### 5.1 Informational Gradient

Define a discrete informational gradient:

\[
\nabla I \sim \frac{\Delta I}{l_{\min}}
\]

Information propagates to minimize gradients, subject to the saturation bound \( I_{\max} \).

---

### 5.2 Informational Action

At large scales, the discrete dynamics admit an effective action:

\[
S_I = \sum_{\text{voxels}} \left[ \alpha (\nabla I)^2 + \beta I \right] l_{\min}^3 t_{\min}
\]

In the continuum limit:

\[
S_I \rightarrow \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \, \mathcal{L}_I
\]

## 6. Emergence of Geometry

The effective metric \( g_{\mu\nu} \) encodes the coarse-grained connectivity of informational exchange.

Regions of higher informational density reduce effective transfer rates, producing an emergent curvature of spacetime.

## 7. Emergent Gravitation

### 7.1 Informational Stress-Energy Tensor

Define:

\[
T_{\mu\nu}^{(I)} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta \mathcal{L}_I}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
\]

This tensor represents informational density and flux.

---

### 7.2 Continuum Limit and Einstein Equations

In the limit:

\[
l_{\min} \to 0, \quad t_{\min} \to 0, \quad \frac{l_{\min}}{t_{\min}} = c
\]

the effective action becomes:

\[
S_{\text{eff}} = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left( \frac{1}{16\pi G_{\text{eff}}} R + \mathcal{L}_I \right)
\]

Variation yields:

\[
G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G_{\text{eff}} T_{\mu\nu}^{(I)}
\]

## 8. Origin of the Gravitational Constant

Dimensional analysis and coarse-graining imply:

\[
G_{\text{eff}} \sim \frac{l_{\min}^2 c^3}{I_{\max}}
\]

Using \( I_{\max} = c / t_{\min} \):

\[
G_{\text{eff}} \sim l_{\min}^2 c^2 t_{\min}
\]

Thus, \( G \) emerges as an effective macroscopic coupling determined by sub-Planckian informational structure.

## 9. Black Holes as Informational Saturation

A black hole corresponds to a region where voxels reach \( I_{\max} \).

Further information cannot propagate radially outward and is redirected along alternative causal paths, producing:

- An informational horizon
- Area-scaling of entropy
- Trapped causal trajectories

The horizon arises where all outward informational channels are saturated.
